01 / AEROSPACE HARDWARE

Scratch-Built Mid-Power Sounding Rocket

IN PROGRESS / UNLAUNCHED
01 Design → 02 Simulation → 03 Fabrication → 04 Avionics → 05 Recovery → 06 Flight Testing

After flying commercial Estes model rocket kits and losing them to excessive parachute drift and wadding ignition, I embarked on designing, simulating, and fabricating a custom mid-power vehicle from scratch. This project is actively in progress: vehicle geometry and stability have been simulated in OpenRocket, custom components are modeled in SolidWorks and printed on my Bambu Lab A1, and a custom Raspberry Pi Pico telemetry payload is currently undergoing bench testing. The custom vehicle has not yet flown; it is being prepared for a sanctioned rocketry club launch in the Arizona desert.

01 Preliminary Flight Tests (Commercial Estes Kits) & Failure Analysis

Before committing to custom fabrication, I flew commercial Estes kits (Alpha 3 and Hi-Flier) to gather field operations experience. The failure modes observed across these flights—excessive wind drift under parachutes, ejection charge wadding burn-through, and launch rod exit dynamics—directly dictated the engineering requirements for the custom scratch-built vehicle.

Preliminary Test Flight • Freestone Park
Estes Hi-Flier • Flight #1 (A8-3 Engine)
Successful flight to ~400 ft apogee. Streamer recovery maintained touchdown within ~30 ft of the pad, demonstrating the drift trade-off compared to parachute recovery.
Ascent Tracking • Failure Mode
Estes Hi-Flier • High-Altitude Ascent
Flight #2 suffered wadding burn-through from hot motor ejection gases, directly motivating the custom flameproof Nomex recovery piston design.

Custom Rocket Stability & Mass Distribution
Stability calculations place the Center of Pressure (28.01") safely behind the Center of Gravity (24.12") to achieve a 1.49 cal static stability margin without adding dead nose weight.

Flameproof Piston Recovery

Recovery Mechanics
Inspect Recovery Rig

To permanently prevent wadding burn-through experienced on Estes flights, designed a piston recovery mechanism. A Nomex piston pushes the 24" parachute out of the recovery tube while protecting the electronics from hot motor gases.

Separation Method:Mid-Joint Nomex Piston
Canopy:24" Ripstop Nylon Parachute
Shock Tether:12-ft Tubular Elastic Cord

SolidWorks CAD ECE 103 • Grade A

Constrained 10-Component Dynamic Assembly

Modeled 10 discrete mechanical parts from dimensioned paper sketches to a fully constrained, functional assembly: Front Grill, Back Grill with pattern cuts, Aerodynamic Fan Blades, Center Hub, Motor Housing, On/Off Knob, Shaft, Frame Ring, Vertical Stand, and Weighted Base.

  • Applied concentric, coincident, and rotational mechanical mates to simulate smooth 360° blade rotation with zero interference.
  • Calculated clearances between the rotating fan blades and spherical housing to eliminate blade-cage contact under dynamic rotation.
  • Generated comprehensive 2D manufacturing drawings with ANSI dimensioning, datum references, and standard tolerances.
  • Embossed custom nameplate on the base and produced multi-angle photorealistic renders in SolidWorks PhotoView.
