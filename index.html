01 / AEROSPACE HARDWARE

Scratch-Built Mid-Power Sounding Rocket

FIRST TEST FLIGHT COMPLETE • ROCKET RECOVERED INTACT
01 Design → 02 Simulation → 03 Fabrication → 04 Avionics Bench → 05 Recovery → 06 Flight Testing

After flying commercial Estes model rocket kits and running into practical issues like burned wadding and wind drift, I designed, simulated, and built a 36.4" scratch-built model rocket from the ground up. I modeled the airframe and internal components in SolidWorks, ran flight simulations in OpenRocket to set a 1.60 cal stability margin, and 3D-printed custom couplers and the nose cone on my Bambu Lab A1. On the scale, the empty airframe weighed in at 309g (modeled at 326g with estimated avionics weight). On September 19, 2026, I completed the maiden test flight on an Estes D12-3 motor at Freestone Park, launching with an empty payload bay to focus the test on basic vehicle flight performance. The rocket completed a clean flight, deployed its parachute near apogee, and was recovered intact on the grass with no visible structural damage.

I started by flying commercial Estes kits to see how model rockets handle on the field. The problems I encountered—burned wadding and excessive drift—directly shaped how I designed the recovery system, selected fin geometry in OpenRocket, and built the airframe.

I flew commercial Estes kits to see pad dynamics and recovery firsthand. Hot motor gases burned right through standard recovery wadding, and desert breezes easily carried the rockets away. That showed me I needed a positive Nomex piston and realistic drift estimates.

Testing Notes & Instrumentation Limitations

Tripod Camera Overheating: The dedicated tripod camera overheated in the ~100°F Arizona heat and failed to record, so the available video for analysis came from handheld phone footage.

Velocity Estimate Excluded: Video analysis produced an estimated velocity of 6.025 m/s shortly after leaving the launch rod, compared with 13.8 m/s predicted by OpenRocket. However, the video analysis was not reliable enough to determine whether this difference reflects the actual flight, so I excluded the velocity estimate from the main simulation comparison.

Next Steps: Flight 1 confirmed that the airframe geometry, 1.60 cal stability margin, motor retention, and Nomex parachute deployment work as expected in flight. The next milestone is completing the flight computer—wiring the BMP390 barometer and MPU-6500 IMU to the Raspberry Pi Pico, building the payload bay mounting sled, and recording onboard altitude and acceleration on Flight 2.

Predicted Trajectory Replay & Simulation Readout

OpenRocket generates detailed numerical simulation output and standard 2D plots. This client-side tool transforms those CSV exports into an interactive 3D flight replay over calibrated satellite terrain to evaluate predicted trajectory turnover and landing dispersion.

  • Simulation Data Parser & Interpolation: Ingests raw OpenRocket CSV files with automatic delimiter and event detection. Uses binary-search sample lookup and linear interpolation (lerp) to generate smooth 60 FPS replay from discrete 10–50 ms simulation samples at 0.25×–10× playback.
  • Calibrated Launch Environment: Maps a 4095×4095 px aerial orthophoto of Freestone Park (0.1247 m/px, 510.7 m domain) to OpenRocket's coordinate conventions (+X East, −Z North), providing a calibrated visualization environment with 50 m range rings.
  • Procedural Vehicle Attitude Visualization: Procedurally aligns the rendered airframe along the instantaneous trajectory velocity tangent (ΔP/Δt) through boost and apogee turnover, deploying a 12-line parachute with modeled descent pendulum oscillation.
  • Simulation Data HUD & Procedural Audio: Displays predicted altitude, velocity, acceleration, thrust, drag, and downrange drift. Uses Web Audio API synthesis to modulate motor rumble from thrust (N) and airflow rush from total velocity (m/s).
  • Pre-Loaded Datasets: Pre-configured with simulation models for Ashton's sounding rocket builds, including the Freestone Park Estes D12-3 maiden flight baseline and the Aerotech F32T-6 mid-power upgrade trajectory.

The Core Invariance Experiment

Electric Potential (V) is a spatial property established by the source-charge configuration, while Electric Potential Energy (U) also depends on the test charge. This simulation demonstrates that distinction through a controlled parameter experiment.

The Fundamental Invariance Principle:
Source • Position Fixed Q = +3.0 μC r = 3.0 m
Electric Potential (Field) V ≈ +7.25 kV UNCHANGED
Test Charge q Varied • U = qV
q = −3.0 μC → U ≈ −21.75 mJ q = 0 → U = 0 q = +3.0 μC → U ≈ +21.75 mJ
  • Invariant Demonstration: For fixed source charge Q and spatial position r, varying test charge q leaves modeled potential V invariant (≈ +7.25 kV), while stored system energy (U = qV) scales proportionally from −21.75 mJ to +21.75 mJ and reverses sign with charge polarity.
  • Softened Potential Kernel: Formulates electrostatic potential using a softened Plummer-type kernel V(r) = keQ / √(r² + a²) with core radius a = 2.2 m (Coulomb constant ke = 8.98755 × 109 N·m²/C²), removing the r = 0 singularity while approaching classical 1/r behavior for r ≫ a.
  • Velocity Verlet Numerical Integration: Particle dynamics are integrated in the horizontal xz-plane under spatial gradient forces (E = −∇V, F = qE) via Velocity Verlet; numerical tests showed total mechanical energy drift below 0.1% over tested simulation intervals.
  • Bound States & Precession: In the modeled attractive potential with U(∞) = 0, negative total mechanical energy (Etot < 0) forms precessing rosette trajectories rather than closed Keplerian ellipses due to departure from pure 1/r symmetry at small radius.
  • Self-Contained Application: Single-file web application with embedded libraries (Three.js, KaTeX) and no build step, featuring a 6-step guided tour, 3D raycast charge manipulation, and engineering prefix auto-formatting (kV, mJ, μJ).
