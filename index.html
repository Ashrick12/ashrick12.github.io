Independent Engineering Project

Scratch-Built Mid-Power Sounding Rocket

Airframe CAD • OpenRocket 6-DOF Sim • Bambu Lab 3D Printing • RP2040 Avionics (In Development)
In Progress • Unlaunched

After flying commercial Estes model rocket kits and losing them to parachute drift and wadding burn, I decided to design, simulate, and build a custom mid-power rocket from scratch. This project is actively in progress: the vehicle geometry and stability have been simulated in OpenRocket, custom aerodynamic parts are modeled in SolidWorks and being 3D printed on my Bambu Lab A1, and a Raspberry Pi Pico telemetry package is currently being developed. The custom vehicle has not yet been launched; it is being prepared for a future club launch.

In-Progress Subsystems & Prototyping

🚀 Aerodynamic Simulation (OpenRocket)

In my initial OpenRocket model, stability was marginal (0.745 cal). I redesigned the fin set with a 5.0" sweep length and 4.0" span (51.3° sweep angle) in aircraft birch plywood, shifting the Center of Pressure aft to 28.01" to achieve a simulated 1.49 calibers static margin with zero dead nose weight.

Simulated Apogee: 268 m (~880 ft on F32 motor)
Simulated Static Margin: 1.49 cal
Simulated Touchdown: 5.76 m/s (under 24" chute)

🪂 Nomex Piston Recovery Design

On my second Hi-Flier flight, the wadding failed and caught fire. To fix this on the custom rocket, I designed a piston recovery setup where a flameproof Nomex cloth acts as a mechanical piston, pushing the 24" parachute out while physically sealing the forward bay from hot motor ejection charges.

Design: Mid-joint separation with Nomex piston
Canopy: 24" ripstop nylon parachute
Status: Parts ordered; assembly planned

⚙️ Payload Carrier CAD Modeling

Modeled the central payload section in SolidWorks. Instead of gluing separate couplers, I integrated upper and lower shoulders directly into the printed body. Replaced a simple hook with a 4-legged cross-arch on the bulkhead floor to distribute parachute shock tension evenly across solid printed perimeter walls.

Tool: SolidWorks 3D CAD

Academic Course Project

10-Part Parametric SolidWorks Desk Fan Assembly

Mechanical design course project (ECE 103 — Grade A) emphasizing parametric modeling, dynamic rotational mates, clearance checking, and 2D ANSI manufacturing drawings.

ECE 103 • SolidWorks CAD

Constrained 10-Component Dynamic Assembly

Designed and modeled 10 components from initial dimensioned sketches to a fully constrained functional assembly: Front Grill, Back Grill with pattern cuts, Aerodynamic Fan Blades, Center Hub, Motor Housing, On/Off Knob, Shaft, Frame Ring, Vertical Stand, and Weighted Base.

  • Applied concentric, coincident, and rotational mechanical mates to simulate smooth 360° blade rotation with zero interference.
  • Calculated clearances between the rotating fan blades and spherical housing to prevent blade-cage contact.
  • Generated comprehensive 2D manufacturing drawings with ANSI dimensioning, datum references, and standard tolerances.
  • Embossed custom nameplate on the base and rendered multi-angle views in SolidWorks PhotoView.
